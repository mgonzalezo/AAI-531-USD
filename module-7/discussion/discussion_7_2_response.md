# Discussion 7.2: Envisioning the Future of AI

## Response

**Introduction**
AI's trajectory over the next 50 years holds transformative potential, but its benefits will not materialize equitably without deliberate governance.

**Key Points**
In telecommunications, AI-driven network orchestration — particularly in Open RAN and 6G architectures — will enable real-time spectrum optimization, predictive maintenance, and adaptive quality-of-service at scales no human operator can sustain, directly expanding connectivity access in underserved regions (Letaief et al., 2022). Autonomous vehicles represent a parallel shift: AI routing and fleet coordination are projected to reduce traffic fatalities by up to 90% while dramatically lowering urban emissions (Fagnant & Kockelman, 2015). However, the greatest risk is AI sovereignty — the absence of binding international accountability frameworks governing who controls foundational models and who bears liability for harmful outputs. The concentration of AI capability in a small number of private actors, without enforceable public-interest obligations, structurally undermines democratic oversight (Dafoe, 2018).

**Conclusion**
What excites me most is AI's potential to make high-quality connectivity and mobility genuinely universal — but realizing that potential requires sovereignty frameworks before capability outpaces governance.
