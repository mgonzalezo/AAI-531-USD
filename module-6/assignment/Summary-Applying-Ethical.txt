Summary
Applying Ethical Frameworks to AI Implications: Different philosophical frameworks offer distinct ways of understanding AI ethics, such as Utilitarianism, which focuses on producing the greatest good for the greatest number, and Deontology, which emphasizes duty and following universal moral rules regardless of the outcome. Other lenses include Virtue Ethics, focusing on the character of the developer, and Ubuntu, a collectivist framework emphasizing that "I am because we are," which prioritizes communal well-being over individual gain.

Analyzing Responsibility through Case Studies (Deepfakes): The transcript uses Deepfakes as a case study to evaluate moral implications. Through a Utilitarian lens, the harm of spreading misinformation might outweigh the creative benefits, while a Deontological perspective would focus on the inherent wrongness of deception and the violation of an individual's right to their own likeness.

Assessing Global Governance and Inclusive Approaches: Ethical approaches to AI must be global and inclusive, moving beyond Western-centric views to incorporate diverse perspectives like Ubuntu. This inclusivity is necessary to address the high-profile cases of harm—such as bias in loan rejections or facial recognition surveillance—that result from design flaws or malicious misuse.

Transitioning from Theory to Practical Governance: To bridge the gap between abstract theory and professional practice, three main approaches are identified:

Principles-based approach: Defining universal, abstract guidelines like fairness, transparency, and privacy (e.g., the Montreal Declaration).

Ethical by Design: Embedding ethics directly into the system through co-design with stakeholders, technical auditing, and human-in-the-loop strategies.

Ethical Consciousness: Cultivating a culture of awareness and responsibility among developers and decision-makers, drawing from established business ethics.

Responsibility in Development and Deployment: AI ethics has emerged out of necessity due to concerns regarding the impact of machine learning on daily life. Practitioners have a responsibility to recognize that even non-malicious intentions can lead to systemic harm, necessitating the use of ethical frameworks to evaluate and mitigate risks before deployment.
